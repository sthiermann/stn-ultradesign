# Task flow, visible work and proportion

**Research date: 2026-09-16.** This original synthesis informs [task-flow design](../../skills/stn-ultradesign/references/task-flow-design.md) and its [record template](../../skills/stn-ultradesign/assets/task-flow.template.md). It investigates clear navigation, contextual investigation, live information and proportion. It contains no private application observations, third-party skill material or copied product assets.

## Main finding

A reduced interface is useful when it reduces competing structure while retaining the information and routes needed for the task. Fewer visible elements, fewer clicks and a familiar material treatment are insufficient evidence. The operational method traces the first meaningful result, object context, supporting detail, permitted action and return. Before presentation, a concept must expose which critical paths are actually reviewable and which remain incomplete.

This is our synthesis, not a worldwide standard or a claim that the named products always provide superior UX. Product documentation establishes vendor behavior. Empirical aesthetic studies address their own stimuli and methods, not the correctness of a business application.

The skill consequently asks whether users can see how to inspect an object, reach its statistics/history, change a relevant period and return without losing context. These are conditional task questions, not a requirement to add every diagnostic tool to every application. Streaming, refresh, pause and collection are separate concepts; their actual implementation must be verified.

## What the golden ratio evidence supports

The golden ratio is approximately 1.618. It can suggest an arrangement to compare, but cannot certify composition or usability.

[Russell's 2000 study of judgment scale and context](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10742842/) reports two experiments in which preferred rectangle proportions changed with instructions and imagined object context. **Access: original abstract through PubMed's indexed record; full methods and data were not retrieved.**

[A second Russell paper, December 2000](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/p3037), reports results closer to the golden section in context-free shape conditions but different proportions for painting conditions. **Access: publisher abstract; full text requires access and was not reviewed.** This is more qualified than either claiming a universal preference or claiming the ratio can never be useful.

[Salera, Vallebella, Iosa and Pecchinenda, 2024](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1721721), report an implicit-association experiment with 100 participants and mixed explicit preference results. **Access: original abstract in the authors' university repository; linked PDF retrieval returned 403, and the publisher page failed to retrieve.** No full-method assessment or replication was performed.

None of these retrieved studies measures business-UI task completion. Our operational conclusion is therefore bounded: compare proportions on the actual content and task, preserving useful image aspect ratios, reading width, comparison space and accessible controls. A phi-based candidate may be retained if it helps; forcing every region into that ratio is unsupported. Do not describe a spacing grid, proximity principle and the golden ratio as interchangeable rules.

## Acceptance and remaining evidence

The new method adds a critical-task matrix and a feedback-to-change record. It asks reviewers to distinguish a visible primary entry from a shortcut, a working detail from a placeholder, a paused display from stopped collection, and a returned context from a fresh landing page. It requires scope omissions to remain explicit and routes full audits back to exhaustive usage/transition coverage.

A proportion or workflow comparison should keep task, fixture, role, input, viewport, theme and relevant preferences comparable. Observe correct outcomes, wrong turns, context loss, reading burden and recovery as well as interaction counts. A designer walkthrough does not establish actual user comprehension. No fresh application run, user study or benchmark of these new instructions was completed during this research. They improve the method's explicitness; their effect still requires application evidence.


## Additional reference observations during concept development

The official [Linear redesign account](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui) and [release article](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-03-20-new-linear-ui) were read. The release article's sidebar and navigation-header images were inspected in the browser: scope grouping, aligned headers, local actions and restrained separators provided specific hierarchy references. These are official published images, not an interaction audit of the signed-in product. The design account's emphasis on whole views and real working conditions informs our comparison process; it does not prove our implementation quality.

Apple's [Design foundations from idea to interface](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/359/) was read through its transcript, and playback frames in the Navigation chapter were inspected (around 4:55–5:48). The visible tab-bar demonstration distinguishes principal destinations and a prominent action. This limited observation is not a full-video review, native-device test or a rule to put an action in every product's navigation. It reinforces evaluating structure and the next useful step before adding material effects.

For settings, Apple's [Split views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/split-views) and [Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars) explain simultaneous navigation and content; Microsoft's [NavigationView](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/controls/navigationview) describes adaptive presentation. The HIG content was available through indexed primary text. Keeping global navigation beside a local settings list is our conditional application of these relationships, not a vendor requirement or universal worldwide standard. Compare direct peer switching with repeated returns through a directory, while preserving a compact sequential mode.
